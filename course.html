01 · WHY COMPUTE?

Experiment and simulation work together

Experiments measure observables under real conditions. Computational chemistry supplies atomistic models, tests mechanisms, estimates properties, and helps interpret data. Predictions remain conditional on the model, method, basis set, and environment.

Measurement, sample preparation, instrument limits, environmental effects.

02 · QUANTUM MECHANICS

What does the Schrödinger equation describe?

The time-independent equation, \(\hat H\Psi=E\Psi\), relates a Hamiltonian operator to a stationary state. The wavefunction contains the quantum state; \(|\Psi|^2\) gives a probability density under the usual interpretation.

Ĥ Ψ = E Ψ

Hamiltonian · wavefunction · energy eigenvalue

One-electron density-like illustration

Why is many-electron chemistry hard?

For N electrons, the full spatial wavefunction depends on 3N spatial coordinates (plus spin variables). Electron–electron interactions couple their motions. The equation still applies; the practical challenge is solving it accurately at useful cost.

03 · MANY-ELECTRON SYSTEMS

Wavefunction versus electron density

For a ground-state system, DFT uses the three-dimensional electron density \(n(\mathbf r)\) as its central variable rather than the full many-electron wavefunction. This makes practical calculations possible, although the exact exchange-correlation functional is unknown.

Many-electron wavefunction

High-dimensional object; electron correlations are encoded in its structure.

Electron density

3D distribution in real space; central quantity in ground-state DFT.

05 · SELF-CONSISTENT FIELD

SCF is a feedback loop

Start from a guess density, build an effective potential, solve the Kohn–Sham equations, generate a new density, and repeat until the chosen convergence criteria are met.

06 · BASIS SETS

Basis sets are the language of orbitals

Orbitals are represented using a finite basis. Larger and more flexible basis sets generally increase computational cost and can improve accuracy, but the best choice depends on the system and target property.

Minimal

Few functions · fast · limited flexibility

Split valence

More flexibility for bonding

Triple-zeta + polarization

Richer representation · higher cost

Examples: STO-3G, 6-31G(d,p), def2-SVP, def2-TZVP. Diffuse functions may be important for anions and excited states.

07 · STRUCTURE & VIBRATIONS

Geometry optimization and frequency analysis

Geometry optimization searches for a stationary point on the potential-energy surface. A harmonic frequency calculation characterizes local curvature at that structure.

Minimum

All real vibrational frequencies for a typical non-linear molecule at a true minimum.

Transition state

Typically one imaginary frequency corresponding to the reaction coordinate.

Very small imaginary frequencies may arise from numerical issues or floppy modes. Inspect the mode and re-check convergence rather than blindly classifying every negative value.

10 · CONCEPTUAL DFT

Electrochemical descriptors

Vertical ionization potential and electron affinity are most consistently estimated from total-energy differences with compatible geometries and methods.

IP E(N−1) − E(N)Ionization potential
EA E(N) − E(N+1)Electron affinity
η (IP − EA) / 2Hardness (one convention)
ω μ² / (2η)Electrophilicity; convention-dependent
Do not equate −εHOMO with IP or −εLUMO with EA automatically. State the definition and method used in any report.
